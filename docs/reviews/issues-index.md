## feasibility -> C:\Työt\Koodaus\Planewar\docs\reviews\C1-feasibility-and-risk.md
The architecture's shape is sound (authoritative SoA sim, one control path, tiered codec, baked terrain, data-driven content), but five things will not work as written:
1. Offline Skirmish on web and mobile runs the whole server sim, 7 bots and the renderer on one thread. No document budgets it and no spike tests it.
2. The server tick budget does not close. Projectile-terrain tests, A* queries and per-client codec work are unbudgeted or underestimated, and S-SIM-1 measures stubs on a desktop core instead of the shared-vCPU VPS.
3. CF-transported gun slugs miss a visually aimed co-altitude target by 13 m at 400 m and 53 m at 800 m.
4. The CF model has no lift: level cruise needs about 29 deg angle of attack, and 03 and 05 contradict each other on landing attitude.
5. GDScript chunk meshing on single-thread web and mobile cannot keep up with boost-speed Chase flight.

Other majors: the predicted ship renders at 30 Hz tick poses, four of eight start slots sit on cube-face seams, contract drift between 02/03/04/05/07, test gates that cannot pass or will flake on shared runners, backend ops load including a moderation console that is not available as specified, 08's scope arriving before core feasibility, and no iOS profiling path.

The bandwidth proof checks out arithmetically; only the POS_SMALL range and dead-reckoning details are wrong. The review includes a re-baselined tick budget (26.5 ms before measurement), a risk register, and the top 5 risky assumptions mapped to amended or new spikes (S-SIM-1, the new S-WEB-2, S-FLIGHT-1, S-WORLD-1/S-WEB-1, S-NET-1 with real browsers).
